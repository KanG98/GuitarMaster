# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Guitar learners practicing at home. Hobbyist to intermediate guitarists who use GuitarMaster as their daily practice companion. They sit down with their instrument, open the app, and move through songs, chord practice, ear training, rhythm drills, and a metronome. They want tools that stay out of their way so they can focus on the instrument.

## Product Purpose

GuitarMaster is an all-in-one guitar practice companion. It centralizes the tools a guitarist needs for daily practice: a song library with tab viewing and YouTube playback, chord reference and quizzes, ear training, rhythm exercises, a metronome, and practice stats. It replaces scattered physical tools (chord books, metronomes, sheet music binders) with a single focused interface.

Success is measured by daily use: the guitarist opens it, practices, and returns tomorrow.

## Brand Personality

Focused, warm, encouraging. Like a patient guitar teacher who keeps the lesson on track but makes you feel good about showing up. The interface should feel supportive without being saccharine, and professional without being cold. Progress is celebrated quietly; the main reward is the practice itself.

## Anti-references

- **No generic enterprise dashboards.** Cold, corporate analytics UIs with sterile data tables and chart-heavy layouts. Stats should feel human and encouraging, not like a quarterly report.
- **No gamification fluff.** No cartoon mascots, no over-the-top celebrations, no virtual currencies or flashy badges. Progress tracking exists to inform and encourage, not to manufacture engagement.
- **No cluttered pro-audio interfaces.** No intimidating DAW-like panels with dozens of tiny knobs. Every tool should be immediately usable by an intermediate guitarist.

## Design Principles

1. **The instrument is the focus.** The UI gets out of the way. When practicing, chrome recedes. The guitarist should be thinking about their fingers on the fretboard, not the interface.
2. **Show up and play.** Every tool surfaces its core action immediately. No wizards, no multi-step setup. Open ear trainer: click a note. Open metronome: press space.
3. **Encourage through clarity.** Progress data should motivate, not judge. Stats are presented as a friendly nudge to keep going, not a report card.
4. **One thing at a time.** Each tool does its job well. The song view is for songs. The metronome is for tempo. Mode switching within a tool stays shallow.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- **WCAG 2.1 AA** target. Sufficient color contrast, keyboard navigation for all interactive tools, and screen reader support for quiz modes and feedback.
- **No color-only information.** Quiz correct/incorrect, active state, and selected items must use at least one non-color indicator (icon, text label, shape change).
- **Keyboard shortcuts** for all practice tools (already partially implemented: space for metronome toggle, arrow keys for quiz navigation, number keys for ear trainer).
